About the Mission

This CubeSat mission is an independent, hands-on effort to design and build a small satellite system from the ground up. The goal isn't just to assemble hardware, but to understand how all the pieces of a real spacecraft work together - power, sensors, onboard software, and communications - and to prove that they can survive and operate in a near-space environment.

The mission focuses on collecting environmental data and sending telemetry back to a ground station in real time. Everything is being designed, tested, and integrated as a complete system, with the same mindset used in professional aerospace projects, just on a much smaller and independent scale.

Once ground testing is complete, the payload will be flown on a high-altitude weather balloon. This acts as a practical stand-in for space conditions. At altitude, the system will experience extreme cold, low pressure, and increased radiation - all of which can affect electronics, sensors, batteries, and communications. The flight will be used to verify that the sensors still produce reliable data, the onboard software behaves as expected, and the communication link remains stable throughout the mission.

This project shows what is achievable when real aerospace engineering practices are applied at an independent, small-scale level.

About the Engineer

The CubeSat mission is led by Natasha Phillips as an independent, systems-level engineering project. She designed and built the spacecraft architecture, focusing on embedded software, sensor integration, and the communications pipeline to ensure end-to-end system operation.

The flight system collects environmental data and transmits real-time telemetry to a ground station. Telemetry is processed through a custom FastAPI backend, stored in a PostgreSQL database, and displayed on a live web dashboard hosted on AWS for monitoring and post-flight analysis.

All embedded software, sensor integration, telemetry systems, and testing procedures were developed independently. Ground testing and a high-altitude balloon flight will be used to evaluate system performance under low temperatures, reduced pressure, and power constraints.
